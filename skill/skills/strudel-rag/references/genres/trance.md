# Trance

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try approximately 132–140 BPM in straight 4/4, with a steady kick, rolling or offbeat bass, an emotionally legible motif, and a long tension-and-release arc. These locally authored creative defaults are not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Compared with psytrance, foreground melodic development and harmonic arrival rather than densely gated bass and psychedelic detail. Faster or darker choices remain possible when requested.

## Groove and low end

Place kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4, claps on 2 and 4, and open hats halfway between beats. Begin with bass on those offbeats; add a quiet shorter pickup only if it does not blur the kick. Bass releases should finish cleanly around the next kick attack. Let arpeggios provide upper-register motion before making the low end busier. Keep the pulse stable underneath builds and melodic suspensions.

## Palette and harmony

Use a clear pluck or layered lead, restrained pads, and a focused bass. Choose a short minor or major chord progression with economical voice leading. Build the hook from three to five notes and one recognizable interval. A broad lead can supply width, but the motif should also work in a plain, dry sound. Do not confuse a continuous arpeggio with a memorable melody.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish a four-bar kick, bass, and hat groove with clean attack separation.
2. Write a two-bar motif with a pause and an unresolved final note.
3. Answer it over the next two bars with a changed ending that suggests arrival.
4. Add an arpeggio derived from the same harmony, quieter or in another register.
5. Make a contrasting version with fewer notes and longer durations; use this as breakdown material rather than inventing an unrelated theme.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate eight-bar restrained and open versions of the same motif. For a full track, consider 16-bar introductions, 16–32-bar development, a melody-led breakdown, a controlled build, and a resolved return. Lengths are suggestions. Restore bass and kick deliberately instead of stacking every layer on the first return beat. Map musical bars to verified timing; one Strudel cycle is not assumed to be one bar.

## Mix and quality checks

Check lead/pad masking, arpeggio density, and reverb spill across chord changes. Increasing brightness and layer count together can create harshness instead of lift. Preserve contrast: an already maximal opening leaves little room for the main arrival. Confirm the motif survives without transition effects.
