# Deep house

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 116–124 BPM in relaxed 4/4, anchored by a rounded kick, an unhurried bass pocket, spacious extended harmony, and restrained melodic detail. These locally authored creative heuristics are defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Distinguish this approach from brighter house through space and harmonic shading, not simply slower tempo; distinguish it from ambient music by maintaining a tangible dance pulse.

## Groove and low end

Begin with kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4, a soft clap on 2 and 4, and hats halfway between beats. Leave some intermediate subdivisions empty. Let a bass pickup approach beat 1, then answer after beat 2 with a shorter note. Keep the kick's attack clear by controlling the pickup's release. Add a quiet percussion answer near beat 4 only if it reinforces the pocket. Avoid shifting every layer equally when seeking looseness.

## Palette and harmony

Use muted electric-key-like chords, mellow plucks, filtered pads, and a rounded bass. Choose two related seventh- or ninth-colored voicings and move their upper voices minimally. Give chord attacks a rhythmic role even when their tails are long. A single sustained upper note can connect changing harmonies; reserve a distinct register for a small answering motif.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish a four-bar drum groove that remains convincing with only kick, clap, and hats.
2. Write a two-bar bass phrase with a deliberate half-beat or longer breathing space.
3. Place chords in the bass gaps, allowing one voicing to last longer than the other.
4. Create a three-note motif with a recognizable pause; answer it an octave higher only at the phrase end.
5. Build a variation by changing one upper chord tone and removing percussion, retaining the bass rhythm.

## Arrangement and variation

For an eight-bar loop, alternate chord articulation and leave the last bar less crowded. For a full track, consider 16-bar layers, a reduced harmony-led middle passage, and a patient return rather than a huge drop. These lengths are optional. Develop space, register, and filter brightness before adding new parts. Musical bar lengths require verified project timing; they are not automatically Strudel cycles.

## Mix and quality checks

Check chord releases against the next harmony, and keep pad lows separate from bass. Excessive reverb can disguise weak voicing and smear syncopation. Avoid making every sound dark: the groove still needs clear transient landmarks. Check that the sparse version retains identity without a lead.
