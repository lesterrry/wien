# Electro

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 115–135 BPM in 4/4: robotic broken beats, syncopated synth bass, terse machine motifs, and controlled timbral changes. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. This is electro, not electro house: emphasize broken kicks and bass conversation, not four-on-floor drops.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Try kicks on 1, the offbeat after 2, and a quiet pickup before 4; place snares or claps on 2 and 4. Use crisp eighth-note hats with occasional sixteenth-note answers. Remove a kick in bar two to create a recognizable response.

Make bass a second rhythmic voice. Place a short note after the first kick, hold another into a gap, then rest before the downbeat. Keep kick and bass envelopes distinct; shared accents should be deliberate.

## Palette and harmony

Choose dry synthetic drums, focused synth bass, metallic lead, and optional self-made robotic vocal texture. A minor pedal or short modal cell can suffice. Keep modulation repeatable. Compared with general breakbeat, emphasize precise synthetic drums conversing with articulated bass.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish a two-bar broken drum pattern with stable snare landmarks.
2. Write a three-note bass motif with alternating short and held durations.
3. Add a high-register answer using the motif's rhythm or contour, not a competing melody.
4. Vary bass brightness on one recurring accent while retaining pitch and timing.
5. Create an answering phrase by removing the final bass note and inserting one dry percussion response.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar phrases with one envelope or percussion change. For a full track, try a 16-bar drum introduction, 32-bar machine dialogue, stripped bass-led contrast, and altered return. Lengths are suggestions. Clean entrances and pauses provide contrast; retain the central bass rhythm.

## Mix and quality checks

Check resonant bass peaks against snare clarity and low-mid headroom. Excessive reverb blurs mechanical articulation; nonstop modulation obscures the motif. Precision need not mean identical velocities. Evaluate low end when monitoring is available; no listening or execution is claimed.
