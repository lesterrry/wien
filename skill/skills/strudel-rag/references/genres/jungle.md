# Jungle

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 160–175 BPM in 4/4: edited breaks, ghost hits, elastic sub, and spacious textures. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Unlike engineered drum and bass, let internal break accents and slice relationships lead.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Begin with principal snares on 2 and 4, a kick near 1, and a syncopated kick between 3 and 4. Preserve quiet touches around main snares: ghost hits are subordinate, not extra backbeats.

Make an alternate bar by repeating a tail slice or leaving a gap before beat 4. Keep the next downbeat legible. Let sustained sub answer drum gaps; shorten notes around busy kicks.

## Palette and harmony

Use self-recorded or clearly licensed breaks and retain required attribution or usage records. Chopping does not establish permission; verify both the recording and any relevant underlying rights before distribution. No named break is required.

Pair rounded sub with one chord stab or pad and optional original vocal-like texture. A minor or modal center connects disparate slices. Preserve source character without uncontrolled noise or low-frequency residue.

## Composition recipe

1. Select a permitted break and identify strong hits, ghost notes, and useful tails.
2. Build a recognizable two-bar phrase before adding edits.
3. Write a short sub motif that answers, rather than copies, the kick sequence.
4. Make a second phrase by moving one pickup and repeating one tail; preserve the main snare landmarks.
5. Add a spacious chord response, then remove any layer that hides the break's internal dynamics.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate an intact phrase with an edited response every four or eight bars. For a full track, consider a 16-bar introduction, 32-bar development, spacious contrast, and return with new slice emphasis. Lengths are suggestions. Preserve motifs rather than making every bar an unrelated fill.

## Mix and quality checks

Check slice-boundary clicks, doubled low end from layered kicks, and inconsistent accent levels. Over-quantization can flatten ghost-note relationships; indiscriminate random slicing can lose the groove. Confirm rights separately from technical asset availability. Audio and code execution are not assumed.
