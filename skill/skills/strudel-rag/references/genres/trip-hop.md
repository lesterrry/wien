# Trip-hop

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Start around 70–100 BPM in 4/4 with a heavy, unhurried broken backbeat. These are creative defaults, not limits. Anchors: weighty drums, a repeating moody motif, spacious bass, and deliberate gaps. This profile emphasizes tension and break-derived phrasing more than generic downtempo; it need not imitate any particular artist or recording.

## Groove and low end

Set snare on beats 2 and 4. Put kick on 1, then add an offbeat pickup before 3 or a delayed answer after it; do not fill every quarter note. Use a quiet ghost snare before one backbeat and vary its presence across two bars. Keep hats restrained and swing only selected subdivisions coherently. A low bass phrase can sustain between kicks, but shorten notes where their tails collide. If using a chopped break, verify asset access and permission rather than naming an assumed sample.

## Palette and harmony

Choose a rounded or dusty drum body, a simple sub or muted bass, and one identity-bearing sound: sparse keys, a plucked figure, or an original vocal-like synth phrase. Minor or modal harmony is a useful option, not a compulsory emotional shortcut. Create unease with register, suspensions, or a held pedal instead of stacking unrelated dissonances. Reserve dub-like delay throws for selected gaps.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish a two-bar kick/snare phrase that remains convincing with hats muted.
2. Add a short bass statement and an answering rest.
3. Write a memorable three- to five-note motif with a distinctive rhythm.
4. Add one sparse harmonic layer; do not obscure the motif with constant chords.
5. Make an alternate phrase by moving one kick and delaying the motif's final note.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, pair 4 bars of the main phrase with 4 bars missing one foreground layer. For a longer track, try an 8-bar motif introduction, 16-bar drum/bass statement, 8-bar reduced passage, and a 16-bar changed return. Suggestion only: adapt to duration and brief. A delay tail into silence or a one-beat drum cut can mark a transition without a large riser.

## Mix and quality checks

- Are the broken kick placements and backbeat still readable?
- Does “dark” come from deliberate musical choices rather than permanent low-pass masking?
- Is there one foreground motif, with space for its answer?
- Are bass, kick decay, and effect feedback bounded?
- Do not describe unplayed output as listened to.
