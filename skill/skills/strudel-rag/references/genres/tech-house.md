# Tech house

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Use approximately 124–130 BPM in straight 4/4, with four-on-floor pressure, a compact bass hook, dry percussion, and deliberate empty space. These are locally authored creative starting points, not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Unlike chord-led house, let bass and percussion carry the identity; unlike techno, favor a clear, conversational groove over continuously evolving timbral pressure.

## Groove and low end

Set kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4 and claps on 2 and 4. Place a hat halfway between beats, then audition a quieter hit just before beat 4. Start a bass answer after beat 1 and repeat its contour after beat 3, changing the final note's length. Keep rests before major kick attacks. Treat kick and bass as an interlocking rhythm, not two independently busy loops. One dry rim or tom accent can supply syncopation without filling every gap.

## Palette and harmony

Choose a short, characterful bass with a readable midrange, one crisp percussion family, and a compact stab or original voice-like fragment. A root-centered phrase with one neighboring pitch may suffice. If using chords, make them punctuation rather than a continuous progression. Distinguish foreground accents from background texture through register and duration, not merely louder levels.

## Composition recipe

1. Make a two-bar drum pattern and identify its strongest non-kick accent.
2. Write a bass motif that answers that accent with two or three short notes.
3. Remove a bass note and compare the resulting space; keep whichever version makes the rhythm clearer.
4. Add one offbeat stab or percussive response, absent during the motif's busiest moment.
5. Create a four-bar variation by changing the final bass duration and omitting one clap, not by replacing the entire groove.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar A and B phrases with one short dropout. For a track, try 16-bar entries, an eight-bar reduction, and a return centered on the same bass hook. Section lengths are suggestions. Build tension by withholding a familiar sound before restoring it; constant risers are unnecessary. Map musical bars to verified project timing rather than assuming a Strudel cycle is a bar.

## Mix and quality checks

Check kick/bass release overlap, low-mid buildup from toms, and sharpness when claps meet hats. Too many percussion layers remove the useful negative space. A bass hook inaudible without sub energy needs a clearer body, not automatically more low-end level. Keep the return from clipping.
