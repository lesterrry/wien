# Synthwave

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 85–115 BPM, straight 4/4, with a steady backbeat. These are starting choices, not genre boundaries. Anchors: pulsing synth bass, a singable electronic lead, and contrasting sustained pads or arpeggios. Retro timbres support the composition; unlike trance, a long accelerating build and dense rolling bass are not necessary. Do not confuse retro character with copying a film theme or existing melody.

## Groove and low end

Start with kick on 1 and 3 and snare on 2 and 4; a four-on-floor variant is also viable. Add steady eighth-note hats with a small end-of-phrase opening. Use bass eighths on a root/octave pattern with at least one rest or held note per two-bar phrase. Keep the bass envelope short enough to preserve kick definition. A wide upper bass layer must not imply several competing sub voices.

## Palette and harmony

Choose an analog-style bass voice, one bright lead, and a restrained pad. A four-chord progression with common-tone voice leading can carry the track; minor keys are an option, not a rule. Use a short arpeggio as accompaniment only when it leaves rhythmic and register space for the lead. Chorus, saturation, and gated-sounding snare ambience are optional colors, not substitutes for a hook.

## Composition recipe

1. Set a stable two-bar drum and bass pulse.
2. Choose four harmonic roots and voice upper chords without doubling the low bass register.
3. Write a four-bar lead with an opening call, an answering phrase, and a breathing point.
4. Repeat the hook with a changed final note or octave response.
5. Add one secondary texture only after the hook works with drums and bass alone.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, use 8 bars with the lead answering differently in bars 5–8. For a full track, try an 8-bar pulse intro, 16-bar hook statement, 8-bar pad-led contrast, 16-bar lifted return, and an outro. Lengths are suggestions. Raise energy with a lead register shift, a brighter pad, or one extra hat layer rather than making all parts louder.

## Mix and quality checks

- Is the lead memorable without the retro effects?
- Are lead and arpeggio competing for the same notes and register?
- Does the pulse remain clear beneath long snare and pad tails?
- Does the return develop the motif rather than introducing an unrelated tune?
- Treat unrendered work as a structural review, not an auditory one.
