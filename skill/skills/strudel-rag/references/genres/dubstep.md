# Dubstep

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 138–144 BPM in 4/4: half-time backbeat, negative space, sub-led phrases, and bass responses. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Aggressive mid-bass is optional, not universal. Distinguish this from faster-backbeat drum and bass or chord-led future bass.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Put the main snare on 3, kick on 1, and an optional kick on the offbeat after 2 or 3. Add sparse eighth-note hats and one sixteenth-note pickup. Half-time describes accents, not halving project tempo.

Let one held sub note answer two shorter notes, with a rest before repetition. Keep kick and sub complementary through placement and envelopes; processing cannot substitute for arrangement.

## Palette and harmony

Choose clean sub, dry drums, one character bass, and a dark chord, bell, or atmosphere. A pedal with neighboring notes can suffice. Develop space and timbre slowly; for an aggressive variant, alternate mid-bass gestures without burying the sub.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish the beat-3 snare and two complementary kick placements.
2. Write a two-bar sub motif with one deliberate silence.
3. Add a midrange response whose rhythm differs from the sub's sustained notes.
4. Introduce one sparse harmonic color or atmospheric cue.
5. Create a four-bar variation by withholding the last bass answer, then restoring it with a changed envelope or register.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate full phrases with stripped drum-and-sub responses over eight bars. For a full track, consider a 16-bar introduction, 32-bar statement, 16-bar contrast, and altered return. Lengths and a dramatic drop are optional. Preserve the rhythmic hook across bass-timbre changes.

## Mix and quality checks

Check the snare's half-time landmark and low-end headroom. Long reverbs consume silence; overlapping bass voices hide articulation. “Heavier” need not mean louder. Evaluate sub phase and mono behavior when monitoring is available; no execution or listening is claimed.
