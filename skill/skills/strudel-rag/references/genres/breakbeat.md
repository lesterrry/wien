# Breakbeat

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 110–135 BPM in 4/4: syncopated kicks, strong backbeat, repeating bass hook, and purposeful drum variation. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Choose midtempo broken drums rather than jungle's rapid edits or electro's specifically robotic synth conversation.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Start snares on 2 and 4, kicks on 1 and the offbeat after 3, and a quieter kick before 2 in alternate bars. Eighth-note hats mark motion; sixteenth-note pickups anticipate accents. Preserve a recognizable kick/snare relationship through fills.

Write bass as a short syncopated phrase with at least one rest. It may reinforce the first kick and answer the later kick rather than doubling everything. Shorten note tails where the next drum attack needs definition.

## Palette and harmony

Choose punchy one-shots or a permitted break, character bass, and a compact synth hook. A pedal with a two-note answer suffices. For chopped breaks, verify recording and underlying rights, redistribution conditions, and attribution; processing supplies no license. Self-recorded material is an option.

## Composition recipe

1. Build two bars of drums with one displaced kick and an unambiguous backbeat.
2. Add a bass motif that uses the drum phrase's empty spaces.
3. Place a short synth or chord answer above the bass register.
4. Make a fourth-bar variation by removing a kick and adding a brief snare pickup.
5. Retain the motif in both sparse and busy versions; reserve the busiest response for a phrase ending.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar A/B phrases with changed drum density or bass endings. For a full track, consider a 16-bar introduction, 32-bar groove, stripped contrast, and return. Lengths are optional. A short drum-only passage can expose the broken rhythm instead of relying on an oversized drop.

## Mix and quality checks

Check kick/bass masking, break-layer phase interaction, and whether ghost hits compete with the snare. Overfilling removes the spaces that make syncopation readable; static hats can flatten an otherwise lively phrase. Confirm sample permissions separately from playback availability. No listening or execution is implied.
