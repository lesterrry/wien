# Hardcore / gabber

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

For this profile, start around 160–190 BPM in 4/4. Faster and slower substyles exist; these are working defaults. Anchors: a forceful distorted four-on-floor kick, tightly controlled tails, clipped rhythmic hooks, and decisive stops or re-entries. “Hardcore” here means electronic hardcore/gabber, not punk. Compared with hardstyle, prioritize fast percussive momentum over a long pitched-kick melodic showcase.

## Groove and low end

Place kick on all four quarter-note beats. Add clap or snare on 2 and 4 only if it remains distinct, then use sparse offbeat hats. At this speed, long kick tails easily overlap the next attack: shorten or reshape the decay before adding more low end. If the kick carries a tonal tail, let it own the sub role; add a separate bass only in deliberate gaps. Restrict kick rolls to short, planned phrase endings.

## Palette and harmony

Use one defined kick sound, a clipped lead or stab, and one contrasting texture. Distortion changes both tone and level; do not assume it is harmless gain. An economical minor/modal motif or a fixed pitched stab can supply identity without continuous chord stacks. Build weight from attack and rhythmic contrast, not from unlimited sub, noise, or effect feedback.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish a four-beat kick pattern with a tail that fits the interval between hits.
2. Add a sparse upper percussion pattern; verify that the kick attack remains clear.
3. Write a one- or two-bar hook with intentional rests.
4. Create a second hook state using displacement or an octave answer, not arbitrary note flooding.
5. Design one short stop and an unmistakable downbeat return before adding transition effects.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate a 4-bar statement with a 4-bar response ending in a brief dropout. For a track, try 8–16 bars of introduction, 16 bars of the main statement, an 8-bar contrast, and a 16-bar changed return. Lengths are suggestions. Stop/start contrast can carry the structure; a melodic breakdown is optional. Avoid sudden unexplained tempo changes to manufacture intensity.

## Mix and quality checks

- Do distorted kick tails obscure subsequent attacks or clash with an added bass?
- Are kick pitches compatible with tonal material when pitch is perceptible?
- Are rolls and subdivisions bounded rather than creating excessive event density?
- Is energy shaped by arrangement, not just permanent maximum density?
- Begin any actual audition at conservative monitoring level; do not claim clipping or loudness compliance without measurement.
