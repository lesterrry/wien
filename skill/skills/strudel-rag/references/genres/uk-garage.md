# UK garage

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 128–138 BPM in 4/4: shuffled subdivisions, displaced kicks, backbeat, and elastic bass. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Choose 2-step here, not all UK garage: unlike house, kick gaps are structural; unlike jungle, break editing need not dominate.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Put snares or claps on 2 and 4. Try kicks on 1, the offbeat after 2, and an occasional pickup before 4; omit one in the answering bar. Add hats on selected eighth-note offbeats and quiet sixteenth-note pickups.

Delay selected weaker subdivisions consistently for shuffle, preserving strong-beat landmarks. Avoid shifting every sound equally. Write a short bass note after one kick and a longer answering note in an open space. Leave a rest before the next downbeat.

## Palette and harmony

Use crisp drums, rounded bass, clipped chord stabs, and an optional original vocal-like fragment. Two related extended chords can supply warmth. Alternate chord and bass accents rather than striking everything together. Sampled vocals require permission, not merely availability.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish the backbeat and a two-bar displaced-kick phrase.
2. Add one shuffled hat figure, comparing its placement against the fixed snare landmarks.
3. Write a syncopated bass motif that answers the kick gaps.
4. Place short chord stabs around that motif and leave one shared silence.
5. Change one bass ending and one hat pickup in bar four; keep the pocket stable.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar phrases with a brief bass or chord dropout. For a full track, try a 16-bar drum introduction, 32-bar groove, 16-bar stripped contrast, and return. Lengths are suggestions. Reveal different chord or vocal fragments while maintaining the bass rhythm.

## Mix and quality checks

Check that shuffle reads as a coherent pocket rather than arbitrary lateness. Long bass tails can smother displaced kicks; sustained pads can erase the clipped chord feel. Too many pickups make a supple groove feel frantic. No listening or execution is claimed.
