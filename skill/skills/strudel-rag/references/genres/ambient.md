# Ambient

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Start without a fixed beat, or choose an internal 50–90 BPM pulse if useful. Meter can be unobtrusive; use 4/4 only as a scheduling convenience. These are creative defaults, not boundaries. Anchors: sustained evolving texture, deliberate space, slow changes in register or spectrum. Unlike downtempo, an audible drum groove is optional; unlike a static preset demonstration, the texture should develop.

## Groove and low end

Begin with no drums. If a pulse is needed, place a quiet event on beat 1 every second 4/4 bar, then omit some repetitions deliberately. Avoid adding a conventional backbeat merely to fill space. Use a restrained low drone or occasional root note, not several overlapping sub layers. Leave a register gap between the drone and the main pad.

## Palette and harmony

Choose two complementary sources: a stable warm body and a thinner moving texture. A pedal tone with two or three slowly exchanged upper notes can supply continuity without a busy chord sequence. Make dissonance intentional and resolve or sustain it consistently. Long envelopes and reverb are options, not proof of atmosphere; do not depend on unverified field recordings or sample names.

## Composition recipe

1. Choose one tonal center and a sparse three-note pitch collection, or define a deliberate non-tonal rule.
2. Establish one sustained layer and its breathing space before adding another.
3. Create a recognizable gesture: an upper note entering, a timbral opening, or a recurring distant pulse.
4. Develop that gesture by register and tone rather than adding constant new notes.
5. Reserve the lowest register for one source; add percussion only if the brief requires it.

## Arrangement and variation

For a short loop, make two 8-bar states with different voicing or brightness and a gentle return. For a longer piece, try 16–32-bar phases: emergence, widening, thinning, return. These lengths are suggestions; free-time material can use an explicitly verified time schedule instead. Overlap transitions without allowing tails to accumulate indefinitely. Density may fall at the emotional peak.

## Mix and quality checks

- Does a recurring gesture remain identifiable as the texture changes?
- Are adjacent sustained notes and effects masking one another?
- Is the low drone restrained rather than permanently filling all headroom?
- Does evolution remain audible in the design without abrupt accidental envelope resets?
- Assess structure only unless audio playback or rendering is actually available.
