# Techno

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Begin around 128–142 BPM in driving 4/4: persistent kick, economical tonal material, interlocking percussion, and gradual timbral evolution. These locally authored creative defaults are not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Compared with tech house, prioritize sustained tension and transformation over a conversational bass hook. Compared with trance, a recognizable identity need not depend on a large melodic release.

## Groove and low end

Place kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4. Add offbeat hats halfway between beats, then a quieter closed-hat pulse if necessary. A clap on 2 and 4 is optional; do not add it merely from habit. Let a short bass pulse answer after beats 1 and 3, or use a controlled low texture around the kick. Avoid combining both approaches at full weight. If using a three-accent percussion grouping, define its repeat length independently and preserve the four-beat kick anchor.

## Palette and harmony

Choose one metallic hit, one narrow tonal stab, and a noise or filtered texture. A pedal tone, two-note interval, or deliberately dissonant cluster can be enough. Develop attack, brightness, resonance, and decay without changing every parameter simultaneously. Keep any low resonant tail intentional; atmospheric layers should not duplicate the bass register.

## Composition recipe

1. Build a four-bar kick-and-hat foundation with a stable accent hierarchy.
2. Add a two-note stab motif with one fixed silence; repeat its rhythm exactly at first.
3. Introduce a complementary percussion pattern that leaves the stab attacks clear.
4. Shape a slow brightness rise across eight bars while preserving pitch and rhythm.
5. Create the response by shortening the stab and removing a percussion hit. Save a new pitch for a larger section change.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, make an eight- or sixteen-bar evolution that returns smoothly to its starting timbre. For a track, try 16–32-bar development blocks, a reduced-pressure passage, and a return with changed density rather than a new tune. These lengths are optional. Avoid simultaneous parameter jumps unless a deliberate rupture is intended. Musical bars require project-specific timing; do not equate them automatically with Strudel cycles.

## Mix and quality checks

Check that resonant sweeps do not create sudden level spikes, and that low tails do not erase kick definition. Constantly adding layers produces congestion, not necessarily development. Ensure the motif remains traceable through sound changes and that secondary rhythmic groupings do not obscure the main pulse.
