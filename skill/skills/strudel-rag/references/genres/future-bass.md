# Future bass

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 135–155 BPM in 4/4: half-time feel, expressive voicings, rhythmic amplitude envelopes, and a singable hook. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Unlike sub-led dubstep, foreground chord rhythm and harmonic movement; large supersaw stacks are optional.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Start with a kick on 1, snare or clap on 3, and a quieter kick after 2. Add sparse eighth-note hats and a short pickup before the next downbeat. Keep fills subordinate to the chord phrase.

Let bass support chord roots with occasional passing notes. Shorten bass around kick attacks. Shape chord attacks into a repeating rhythmic gesture, leaving gaps for drums and melody; continuous loud chords weaken that gesture.

## Palette and harmony

Choose bright chords, rounded bass, clear drums, and a contrasting lead or original vocal-like texture. Try four chords sharing upper notes with modest voice movement. Preserve a distinct melody instead of relying on size. Keep low chord tones away from the sub.

## Composition recipe

1. Write a four-chord progression and a short top-line motif sharing its strongest tones.
2. Give the chords a two-bar rhythm with held attacks, shorter responses, and rests.
3. Fit kick and snare into the remaining spaces, then add root-supporting bass.
4. Shape chord loudness and brightness together where an expressive swell is wanted.
5. Vary the final motif note or chord inversion in the answering phrase; retain its recognizable rhythm.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four bars of full chords with four of reduced voicings. For a full track, consider a 16-bar introduction, 16-bar preparation, 32-bar statement, contrast, and return. Lengths and a drop are optional. Reveal the motif before its largest orchestration.

## Mix and quality checks

Check chord/bass register overlap, sharp upper-mid buildup, and whether pumping obscures the hook. Excessive detuning can blur harmony; excessive chord release can fill intentional rests. Reserve space for the snare. No monitoring or execution is implied.
