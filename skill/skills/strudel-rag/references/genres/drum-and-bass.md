# Drum and bass

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try 168–176 BPM in 4/4: engineered fast drums, controlled sub, contrasting bass phrases, and phrase-end changes. These locally authored heuristics are creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, genre authority, or verified Strudel code. Compared with jungle, prioritize a designed kick/snare chassis over constantly re-cut breaks.

## Groove and low end

Count four quarter-note beats per bar. Try kicks on 1 and the offbeat after 3, with snares on 2 and 4. Add one quiet pickup. Eighth-note hats establish speed; occasional sixteenth-note details add motion. Preserve snare landmarks.

Write one- or two-bar bass statements. Sustain sub through gaps but shorten or duck it around kicks. Let mid-bass answer the sub; avoid incompatible low fundamentals.

## Palette and harmony

Choose punchy drums, sub, character bass, and one atmospheric or melodic layer. A minor pedal with a two-note answer can suffice. Keep distortion and width mainly above the sub region.

## Composition recipe

1. Build a two-bar kick/snare skeleton and mark one intentional silence.
2. Add hats and a single quiet drum pickup; preserve the main accents.
3. Write a short bass contour with an answering rest or note in bar two.
4. Add a compact harmonic or atmospheric hook outside the bass register.
5. Make a four-bar variant by changing only the final bass answer and one drum fill. Retain the original contour.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar A/B phrases, briefly thinning hats or bass. For a full track, consider a 16-bar introduction, 32-bar statement, 16-bar contrast, and altered return. Lengths are suggestions. A half-time-feeling passage need not change transport tempo.

## Mix and quality checks

Check fast-hat masking, kick/bass overlap, and monotonous intensity. Excessive fills erase drive; excess bass layers conceal the motif. Evaluate sub translation and mono compatibility when monitoring is available; no listening or execution is implied.
