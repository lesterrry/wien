# Psytrance

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Start around 138–148 BPM in tightly articulated 4/4, with consistent kick, gated bass, a stable tonal center, and shifting psychedelic midrange gestures. These are locally authored creative defaults, not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Unlike melody-led trance, build interest primarily through rhythmic precision, texture, and call-and-response. Unlike techno's broader repetition, foreground a close-fitting kick/bass engine and quick, contrasting sound gestures.

## Groove and low end

Place kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4. As one starting pattern, divide each beat into four equal sixteenth-note positions: kick at the first, short bass notes at the second, third, and fourth. This is beat notation, not a Strudel cycle grid. Leave audible gaps between bass notes and finish the final release before the next kick. Use restrained offbeat hats and optional claps on 2 and 4. Keep bass timing stable while upper percussion changes.

## Palette and harmony

Choose a compact bass, a squelchy resonant voice, a contrasting dry pluck, and a sparse atmosphere. A root-centered bass with occasional deliberate pitch changes can support richer upper gestures. Give each gesture a bounded register and rhythmic slot. Contrasting envelopes and timbres can sound more psychedelic than arbitrary scale changes or excessive effects on every layer.

## Composition recipe

1. Build two bars of kick and bass, defining note gates and release space before adding other sounds.
2. Add hats that clarify the pulse without duplicating every bass attack.
3. Write a short three-event midrange motif followed by an equally clear silence.
4. Answer that motif with a different timbre in the gap, preserving its rhythmic family.
5. Create an eight-bar development by changing one response's register or filter contour. Reserve bass pitch variation for a deliberate phrase boundary.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, rotate two four-bar call-and-response variants above an unchanged low-end engine. For a track, try 16-bar texture blocks, a short engine dropout, and a return with one new foreground gesture. Lengths are suggestions; an extended melodic breakdown is optional, not compulsory. Keep transformations purposeful rather than constantly random. Map all musical bars to verified project timing instead of assuming bar/cycle equivalence.

## Mix and quality checks

Check each kick/bass handoff, particularly the last bass release before a downbeat. Overlong notes destroy articulation; excessive shortening can lose body. Monitor resonant peaks, competing midrange calls, and stereo effects on low frequencies. A psychedelic surface should not obscure the foundational pulse.

- Strudel examples of resonant filter movement and alternating musical phrases.
