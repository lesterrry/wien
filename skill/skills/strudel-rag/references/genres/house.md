# House

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Start around 120–128 BPM in straight 4/4: steady kick, buoyant offbeat hats, syncopated bass, and soulful chord conversation. These are locally authored creative defaults, not genre rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Compared with tech house, give harmony and expressive phrasing more responsibility; compared with deep house, allow a brighter, more immediate hook.

## Groove and low end

Place kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4, with claps on 2 and 4. Put open hats halfway between beats; add quieter closed hats only where they improve motion. Start the bass after beat 1, answer near beat 2's offbeat, and leave room before the next downbeat. Shorten bass notes around kick attacks rather than relying entirely on processing. Keep a stable backbeat when experimenting with percussion swing.

## Palette and harmony

Choose warm keys, organ-like stabs, or a rounded synth chord voice. A two- or four-chord loop with sevenths or ninths can provide color without constant harmonic movement. Keep common tones between voicings. Let bass establish roots while chords occupy the middle register. Use one brief original melodic or vocal-like response, not several competing hooks.

## Composition recipe

1. Build a two-bar kick, clap, and hat foundation with one restrained pickup.
2. Write a bass phrase whose rests make the kick readable; repeat its rhythmic identity across chord changes.
3. Add a two-note chord-stab rhythm, leaving the second half of one bar open.
4. Answer that gap with a short melodic gesture. In the next phrase, change its ending rather than replacing it.
5. Make an alternate version by removing a chord hit and opening one hat accent.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate two four-bar versions and reserve a small fill for the eighth bar. For a track, try 16-bar drum introduction, 16-bar harmonic statement, a short stripped passage, and a fuller return. Lengths are suggestions. Bring back the same hook with changed register or chord articulation. Musical bars must be mapped to project timing; do not assume one Strudel cycle equals one bar.

## Mix and quality checks

Check that chord tails do not cloud the bass, hats do not dominate the clap, and groove survives without effects. Constant fills can erase the pocket; excessive pads can turn a rhythmic hook into indistinct wash. Preserve headroom when adding the return section.
