# Hardstyle

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try approximately 145–155 BPM in forceful 4/4, anchored by a pitched distorted kick, emphatic accents, dramatic contrast, and a clear melodic or screech-like identity. These locally authored creative defaults are not rules, retrieved evidence, or verified Strudel code. Unlike trance, the kick's sustained pitched body can carry the bass role. Unlike generic hard techno, make that kick's contour and relationship to the hook a deliberate compositional feature.

## Groove and low end

Begin with kicks on beats 1, 2, 3, and 4; add a clap or snare on 2 and 4 only if its attack remains distinct. Use offbeat hats sparingly. Treat the kick as attack plus pitched tail, checking whether a supplied sample has a stable tonal body. If that tail supplies the low end, omit a simultaneous sub line. For separate bass passages, shorten or remove the tail first. Leave space around fills rather than overlapping multiple long kick tails.

## Palette and harmony

Choose one distorted kick family, a contrasting lead or screech, and restrained pads or impacts. Write a simple harmonic movement whose roots can guide kick-tail pitch where appropriate. Transposition changes sample duration and tone unless a verified method compensates; do not assume every kick remains usable at every pitch. Keep melodic voicings above the kick's low body.

## Composition recipe

1. Establish four bars of quarter-note kicks with controlled tail lengths and consistent perceived weight.
2. Write a short hook with a strong interval and a rest, using a plain lead before layering.
3. Align harmonic roots with intentional kick-tail pitches, or hold a compatible pedal note.
4. Add a contrasting screech response only in the hook's gap.
5. Create a second version by changing the hook's final interval and removing one kick before the return. Keep the identity recognizable without continuous fills.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate four-bar hook statements with different kick articulation. For a track, consider a restrained introduction, a melody-led break, a short build, and a 16-bar kick-led statement followed by a contrasting return. Lengths are suggestions. Contrast can come from silence and register, not just louder distortion. Musical bars must follow verified project timing, not assumed Strudel cycle lengths.

## Mix and quality checks

Check kick pitch against harmony, low-end overlap, harsh distortion buildup, and clipping at layered impacts. More drive can reduce attack definition rather than increase impact. Check that transposed kicks do not leave gaps or overrun the next beat. Preserve headroom and avoid adding sub merely because the arrangement feels sparse.

## Reference lookup prompts

- Strudel APIs for sample pitch, playback speed, and envelope duration.
- Strudel examples of distortion, melodic layering, and section dropouts.

Verify syntax, timing, and sound assets in the project and RAG before implementation.
