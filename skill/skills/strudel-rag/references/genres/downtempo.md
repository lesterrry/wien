# Downtempo

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Use roughly 80–110 BPM, 4/4, with a relaxed but legible pocket. These are working choices, not rules. Anchors: an unhurried groove, economical bass, and a slowly changing melodic or textural motif. Compared with ambient, retain a clear rhythmic foreground; compared with trip-hop, a dark, heavy sampled-break character is optional rather than required.

## Groove and low end

Begin with kick on beat 1 and a quieter pickup near beat 3; use snare or rim on 2 and 4. Add eighth-note hats, then remove selected offbeats before adding any ghost notes. A lightly delayed snare feel or gentle swing can help, but keep kick, bass, and main backbeat internally consistent. Place bass with the main kick, then let a short answering note occupy a gap. Avoid sustained sub under every drum tail.

## Palette and harmony

Combine rounded drums, a soft bass voice, one key or plucked chord sound, and a thin atmospheric layer. A two- or four-chord loop with common tones gives motion without demanding constant attention. Use extensions only where voicing leaves room for the motif. Texture and gentle saturation are creative options, not a requirement for simulated vinyl noise.

## Composition recipe

1. Make a two-bar drum phrase with one intentional rest and one quiet pickup.
2. Add a bass contour of two to four notes, matching note lengths to drum gaps.
3. Choose a short chord progression and separate its register from the bass.
4. Introduce a two-bar melodic answer that leaves at least one beat unoccupied.
5. Build a second state by changing voicing or removing percussion, not by doubling every part.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, alternate two 4-bar phrases and change one hat or melodic ending on the second. For a full track, try an 8-bar texture intro, 16-bar groove statement, 8-bar reduction, 16-bar developed return, and a short outro. Lengths are suggestions. Use a bass rest, chord inversion, or brief drum dropout as a transition; avoid a festival-style build unless requested.

## Mix and quality checks

- Is the groove relaxed without losing its pulse?
- Does the motif stay clear when pads enter?
- Are delays and reverb leaving room for the next snare and bass attack?
- Is there meaningful phrase variation rather than an unchanged one-bar loop?
- Keep a structural review distinct from an actual listening check.
