# IDM / experimental electronica

[Genre guide index](README.md)

## Working brief

Try an internal 80–160 BPM clock, or choose another explicitly stated timing scheme. Begin in 4/4 before introducing a contrasting grouping if useful. These are permissive creative defaults, not a definition of a broad label. Anchors: detailed timbral events, controlled rhythmic irregularity, and an identifiable motif or transformation rule. Unlike simply randomizing a drum loop, complexity should have memory and contrast.

## Groove and low end

Establish an anchor kick on beat 1 and a recurring snare or click near beat 3. Build a second, quieter percussion layer with a five-event grouping against the stable bar only after verifying how it repeats. Keep one recognizable accent across changes. Put bass in selected gaps and avoid making every micro-edit a sub event. Dense bursts should be brief and followed by recovery space.

## Palette and harmony

Choose a small coherent set: one low voice, two or three contrasting percussion timbres, and a pitched or textural motif. A limited scale, fixed interval set, or explicitly non-tonal rule keeps selection intentional. Change envelope, register, or articulation to create relationships between sounds. Glitches and granular-like gestures require verified sources and methods, not guessed APIs.

## Composition recipe

1. Write a simple two-bar anchor phrase that can recur unchanged.
2. Define one transformation rule, such as moving one accent by a sixteenth on each repeat before resetting.
3. Add a short pitched motif or repeated timbral gesture that survives that transformation.
4. Create one busy response and one sparse response; bound their event density.
5. Keep the form deterministic initially. If randomness is requested, confine it to a small pitch/timbre set and selected phrase positions.

## Arrangement and variation

For a loop, build an 8-bar sequence: recognizable statement, displacement, brief burst, reset. For a full piece, alternate 8–16-bar transformation blocks with a quieter return to the anchor. Lengths are suggestions. A change of grouping should be audible as a relationship to the established pulse, unless losing that pulse is the explicit aim. Do not apply unrelated changes to every layer simultaneously.

## Mix and quality checks

- Can the motif or transformation rule still be identified?
- Are event density, voice counts, and effect tails bounded?
- Does a complex grouping resolve or intentionally continue across bar boundaries?
- Do sharp transients and low-end bursts leave room for the stable layer?
- Separate code/structural review from any actual audio or performance measurement.
